## 🎤 Speaker Notes (what you say — ~3 minutes)

> *"Let me unpack what I mean by 'the lines have blurred.'*
>
> *For years, we treated three categories of threat as distinct: **cybercriminals** who wanted money, **spies** who wanted secrets, and **soldiers** who wanted disruption. Each had different motivations, different tools, different targets. Our defenses were organized around those categories.*
>
> ***That model is now obsolete.*** *Three fundamental shifts explain why."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 1 — Pre-positioning]**
> *"First: **pre-positioning**. Nation-states are no longer just stealing data. They are **quietly infiltrating critical infrastructure** — energy grids, telecoms, logistics — and staying there, dormant, sometimes for years. The goal isn't immediate damage. It's to have a **loaded weapon** already inside your network the day a geopolitical crisis escalates* [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). *We'll see a concrete example when I discuss Singapore's telecoms."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 2 — AI Asymmetry]**
> *"Second: **AI asymmetry**. Twelve months ago, we talked about AI as an emerging threat. Today, it is a **core operational component** of every serious threat actor. They use it to automate reconnaissance across our entire perimeter, to craft spear-phishing emails that are grammatically perfect and contextually precise, and to scale attacks at speeds our human defenders cannot match* [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). *This is a productivity revolution — for the wrong side."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 3 — Hybrid Model]**
> *"Third: **the hybrid model**. States no longer conduct attacks with their own hands. They orchestrate an ecosystem — elite APTs for the sophisticated work, cybercriminal syndicates for volume, and hacktivists for public-facing intimidation* [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). *This gives them what they want most: **plausible deniability**. When an attack happens, attribution takes months — and by then, the political moment has passed."*
>
> **[Pause — deliver the bottom banner line slowly]**
> *"The consequence for us is simple but profound. **We are no longer defending against criminals looking for a payday. We are operating in a contested geopolitical space** — and the adversary has state-level resources, patience, and strategic intent.*
>
> *That reframes every decision we make: budget, architecture, third-party risk, crisis response. It's the lens for everything that follows in this presentation."*
