# 🎯 Slide 10 — Cyber Resilience First (The Closing Slide)

This is your **decision moment**. Every slide before this was setup. This slide is the ask. It should feel like a **firm, quiet landing** — not a crescendo. Executives make decisions when they feel clarity, not when they feel pressure [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md).

The narrative discipline: **one paradigm shift, three concrete asks, one memorable closing line**.

## 🖼 On-Slide Content

### **Title (top):**
> # Cyber Resilience First
> ### *The 2026 priority — from prevention to survivability*

### **Framing statement (top band, high visual weight):**
> **Pure prevention is no longer sufficient against a nation-state threat with unlimited time and resources.** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
> **The absolute priority for 2026 is the operational capability to maintain degraded business functions during a critical breach.** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)

### **Layout — Three "pillar" cards side-by-side (the concrete asks):**

---

#### 🛡️ **PILLAR 1 — RADICAL ZERO-TRUST ADOPTION**
> ### *Rebuild trust boundaries around identity — human AND machine*

**Concretely:**
- Complete **Non-Human Identity** discovery, inventory & governance
- Extend Zero-Trust principles to service accounts, API keys, workflows [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- Behavioral analytics & Identity Threat Detection layer

**Why now:**
The identity gap is our largest exposed surface and the primary 2026 attack vector.

---

#### ⚙️ **PILLAR 2 — AUTOMATED SECURITY POSTURE MANAGEMENT**
> ### *Compress detection & response cycles from hours to minutes*

**Concretely:**
- Automation of incident response — SOAR capabilities [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- Continuous security posture monitoring & auto-remediation [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- Edge & supply chain visibility program

**Why now:**
Autonomous attacker capability requires autonomous defender capability. Human-in-the-loop response is losing the speed race.

---

#### 🏛️ **PILLAR 3 — DEGRADED-MODE BUSINESS CONTINUITY**
> ### *Operate through a breach, not just recover from one*

**Concretely:**
- Rehearsed **degraded-mode operations** for critical banking functions [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- Nation-state scenario tabletops at ExCo level (incl. deepfake, wiper, edge compromise)
- Cyber-crisis playbook integrating regulator, client, and executive comms

**Why now:**
When — not if — a sophisticated compromise occurs, our ability to *keep serving clients* is the business continuity KPI.

---

### **Closing statement (bottom band, high visual weight):**
> **Cyber risk is no longer an IT concern. It is inextricably linked to geopolitics and to our overarching business strategy.** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)

### **Final line (centered, bold, distinct visual treatment):**
> ## *"The question is no longer if we will be breached. It is whether we will still be operating the day after."*

>
> *Now the question is: **what do we do about it?**"*
>
> **[Point to the framing statement]**
> *"The foundational answer is a paradigm shift — and I want to be direct with this committee about it.*
>
> ***Pure prevention is no longer sufficient*** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). *For twenty years, cyber strategy has been built on the promise that with enough investment, we could keep the adversary out. Against a nation-state with **unlimited time, unlimited resources, and strategic patience** — that promise cannot be kept. Not by us. Not by any institution.*
>
> *So the 2026 priority is a different question. It is not 'how do we prevent every intrusion?' It is: ***'how do we continue operating — serving clients, moving payments, honoring obligations — while under active compromise?'*** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)*
>
> *That is what cyber resilience means. And it requires three shifts."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 1]**
> *"**First: radical Zero-Trust adoption** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md) — extended beyond human users to the machine identities that now dominate our environment. This is where the NHI program I mentioned earlier lives, and it's the single most direct answer to the 'log in, don't break in' problem I showed you on Slide 5."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 2]**
> *"**Second: automated security posture management** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). When adversaries move at machine speed, human-in-the-loop defense loses. This is our answer to autonomous malware and to the compression of detection windows we discussed."*
>
> **[Point to Pillar 3]**
> *"**Third: degraded-mode business continuity** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). This is the most important of the three, and the least discussed. Our current BCP framework assumes recovery from an outage. What we need is the ability to **operate through a compromise** — keep essential banking functions running while remediation happens in parallel. That is a different architectural conversation, and it is where I will need this committee's active engagement in the coming quarters."*
>
> **[Deliver the closing statement slowly and deliberately]**
> *"I want to leave you with one strategic point that reframes everything I've said today.*
>
> ***Cyber risk is no longer an IT concern*** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). *It is inextricably linked to **geopolitics** — because our adversaries are now states. It is inextricably linked to **business strategy** — because the operational, financial, and reputational consequences are enterprise-level, not technical-level. And that means the governance of cyber risk belongs in this room, with this committee — not two floors down.*
>
> **[Pause. Then the final line, slowly]**
> ***The question is no longer if we will be breached. It is whether we will still be operating the day after.***
>
> *Thank you. I'm ready for your questions."*

## 💡 Anticipated ExCo Questions (be ready — this is where the real conversation begins)

**Q1: "What are your top 3 asks from this committee, concretely?"** *(the decisive question)*
> Suggested answer: *"Three specific asks, in priority order. **First**: sanction the NHI discovery and governance program — I'll bring the detailed business case to the next steering. **Second**: endorse the shift in BCP framework from 'recovery from outage' to 'operations under compromise' — this requires ExCo-level engagement because it touches business processes, not just IT. **Third**: commit to a nation-state scenario tabletop exercise at ExCo level in the next six months. The other decisions flow from these three."*

**Q2: "What's the timeline? When do you need decisions?"**
> Suggested answer: *"Decisions on the three asks: **within 60 days** for the NHI program sanction; **within one quarter** for the BCP framework shift; **within six months** for the ExCo tabletop. Delaying any of these does not create an immediate incident — but each month of delay is a month during which the exposure I described today is not being closed."*

**Q3: "You've painted a serious picture. What keeps you up at night specifically?"** *(the informal-but-critical question)*
> Suggested answer: *"Two things, honestly. **First**, the pre-positioning problem — the possibility that a sophisticated adversary is already inside somewhere in our environment, waiting. Because that is what pre-positioning *looks like* when it's working. **Second**, the compression of decision windows in a live incident. Our current crisis governance assumes we have hours to convene decision-makers. Against a fast-moving nation-state incident, we may have minutes. Both are addressable — but neither is addressed today."*

**Q4: "How do we measure success? What's the resilience KPI?"** *(the measurement question — very ExCo)*
> Suggested answer: *"Three measurable KPIs I propose. **RTO in degraded mode** — how quickly can we resume essential functions under compromise, measured in tabletop exercises. **NHI coverage** — percentage of non-human identities under governance, currently under-measured and a key target. **Detection maturity** — mean time to detect a simulated APT-style intrusion, benchmarked against peer institutions. I'll propose the full KPI framework at the next risk committee."*

**Q5: "Is this presentation something we should share more broadly? With the board? With business heads?"** *(the amplification question)*
> Suggested answer: *"Yes to both, in tailored forms. **For the board**: a shorter version focused on strategic risk framing and the three asks — I can prepare it. **For business heads**: a version emphasizing sector-specific exposures — corporate & institutional banking, wealth, retail — because their operational continuity is what resilience actually protects. I recommend both, and I'll propose a communications plan if the committee supports."*

**Q6: "What if you're wrong? What if the threat is overstated?"** *(the healthy-skeptic question — expect it)*
> Suggested answer: *"Fair question, and I want to answer it directly. **The specific incidents I showed you today are documented, attributed, and independently verified**. The trend synthesis is my professional judgment based on the pattern — and reasonable people could disagree at the margins. But even in a scenario where the threat is 30% less severe than I've portrayed, the three investments I'm proposing are **still net-positive** — they close identified gaps, align with regulatory direction, and match peer institution roadmaps. The asks are robust across a range of threat scenarios, not just the worst case."*

**Q7: "How does this align with the ongoing DORA program?"** *(the internal-alignment question)*
> Suggested answer: *"Very tightly. DORA compliance and cyber resilience are the same conversation from two angles — DORA is the regulatory framing, resilience is the operational reality. The three asks I'm proposing all map to explicit DORA requirements: operational resilience testing, third-party risk (including edge and telecom dependencies), and incident response governance. This isn't parallel work — it's the same program, better resourced."*

**Q8: "Is there anything you'd like this committee to do differently in how we govern cyber risk?"** *(the invitation to escalate — take it seriously)*
> Suggested answer: *"Yes, one specific request. Cyber risk currently appears on this committee's agenda quarterly, tied to incident reports. I would propose it appear **strategically at least twice a year** — not driven by incidents, but by threat landscape evolution and roadmap decisions. Today's briefing is the model I'd like to institutionalize. That would meaningfully improve our governance posture and match the practice at our leading peers."*
