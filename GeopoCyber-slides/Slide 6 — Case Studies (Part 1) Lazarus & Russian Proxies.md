#### 🇰🇵 **CASE 1 — LAZARUS (North Korea)**
> ### *Dual-purpose campaign: Cash + IP*
> **Timeframe:** November 2025 [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)

**What happened:**
- **$30.4M** in cryptocurrency stolen from **Upbit** (South Korean exchange) [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- **Simultaneously**, three European defense companies infiltrated via Remote Access Trojans to steal **drone manufacturing IP** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)

**The "so what":**
- **One operation, two revenue streams** — direct state funding + resellable/reusable military IP
- **Confirms the "cybercrime-syndicate-as-state" model** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md) — the boundary between criminal theft and state intelligence has collapsed
- **Speed of laundering:** stolen funds moved through mixers and cross-chain bridges within minutes — recovery window essentially zero

**Executive takeaway:**
> *For a bank, Lazarus is not a hypothetical threat. It is an active, well-resourced adversary with a proven playbook against financial infrastructure.*

---

#### 🇷🇺 **CASE 2 — RUSSIAN PROXIES (Polish Power Grid)**
> ### *Grey-zone hybrid warfare*
> **Timeframe:** December 2025 – January 2026 [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)

**What happened:**
- Coordinated cyberattack on **~30 sites** of the Polish national power grid [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md)
- Executed by **state-backed hacktivist proxies** — not the Russian state directly
- Aligned with the geopolitical context: Poland as **primary logistics hub** for Ukraine support

**The "so what":**
- **Textbook "grey zone" operation** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md) — deliberate ambiguity between military and criminal action
- **Attribution took weeks** — political moment passed before response could be coordinated
- **Critical infrastructure is not "someone else's problem"** — energy disruption cascades into finance, logistics, and payments

**Executive takeaway:**
> *If we finance, insure, or transact in critical-infrastructure sectors, we are downstream of every attack of this kind — whether we're the target or not.*

> **Two attacks. Two motivations (money vs. sabotage). Same underlying model: state intent, deniable execution, systemic impact.**

## 🎤 Speaker Notes (what you say — ~3 minutes)

> *"Let me anchor everything we've discussed so far in two concrete events from the past nine months. Both happened. Both are attributed. Both tell us something specific about how the threat has changed."*
>
> **[Turn to the Lazarus card]**
> *"The first is a **Lazarus** campaign that concluded in November 2025 [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md).*
>
> *In a single quarter, this North Korean group did two things simultaneously. First, they executed a **$30.4 million cryptocurrency theft** from Upbit, one of South Korea's largest exchanges [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). That money went directly to funding the North Korean state — nuclear program, missile program, sanctions evasion. Straight cash.*
>
> *But — and this is the part that reframes how we think about them — **at the same time**, using the same operational tempo, they infiltrated **three European defense companies** to steal **drone manufacturing intellectual property** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). That IP has dual value: North Korea can use it for their own weapons program, and they can potentially sell or trade it.*
>
> *This is what I mean when I say the categories have collapsed. Is this cybercrime? Yes — $30M was stolen. Is this espionage? Yes — defense IP was exfiltrated. Is this state-sponsored activity? Yes — the proceeds fund a sanctioned regime. **All three, in one campaign.***
>
> *For us, as a financial institution, the lesson is direct: **Lazarus is an active, well-resourced adversary with a proven playbook against our exact type of infrastructure.** And the speed at which they now launder stolen crypto — minutes, not hours — means our detection-and-recovery model has to be rebuilt around that reality."*
>
> **[Turn to the Russian Proxies card]**
> *"The second case is entirely different in character — but connected in strategic logic.*
>
> *Between December 2025 and January 2026, a coordinated cyberattack struck approximately **thirty sites of the Polish national power grid** [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). Poland is, as you know, the **primary logistics hub** for Western military and humanitarian support to Ukraine. Every convoy, every rail shipment, every command node passes through Polish infrastructure.*
>
> *The attack was **not attributed to the Russian state directly**. It was executed by **hacktivist proxies** claiming political motivations [[1]](file://Pres-geoPolitique.md). And that is precisely the point. This is what we call a **grey-zone operation** — an act that is unambiguously strategic in intent, but deliberately deniable in execution. By the time attribution was formal, the operational window had closed. The message had been delivered.*
>
> *The lesson for us — and I want this to land — is that **we don't have to be the target to bear the cost**. If regional energy infrastructure goes down, our payment systems slow, our clients in affected sectors face operational stress, our credit exposure changes overnight. **Critical infrastructure attacks are systemic risk events for a bank, whether or not we're on the target list.***
>
> **[Deliver the bottom banner slowly]**
> *"Two attacks. On the surface, they look nothing alike. One is a heist. The other is sabotage. But underneath, the model is identical: **state strategic intent, deniable execution, systemic downstream impact**. That's the pattern we need our defenses — and our crisis response — to be designed around."*

## 💡 Anticipated ExCo Questions (be ready)

**Q1: "Was Upbit's security actually weak, or is $30M just impossible to prevent?"**
> Suggested answer: *"Upbit is one of the better-secured exchanges in the region — this wasn't a case of amateur defenses. Lazarus used a combination of supply-chain compromise of a third-party service provider and social engineering targeting a small number of privileged operators. It's a reminder that **even mature security postures fail when the adversary is patient and well-resourced** — and it's why our own controls have to assume compromise, not just prevent it."*

**Q2: "Have we been targeted by Lazarus specifically?"**
> Suggested answer: *"We have observed Lazarus-attributable reconnaissance activity against our external infrastructure — this is consistent with what every tier-1 bank reports. We have not, to our knowledge, experienced a successful Lazarus intrusion. That said, 'to our knowledge' is doing a lot of work in that sentence — which is why the visibility investments we'll discuss later matter."*

**Q3: "The Polish grid attack — did it actually cause blackouts?"**
> Suggested answer: *"Not widespread blackouts, but **operational degradation** across affected sites and significant emergency response costs. The strategic value for Russia wasn't the outage itself — it was **demonstrating the capability** and **imposing cost** on a NATO logistics hub. That kind of psychological and economic pressure is often the goal, not physical destruction."*

**Q4: "Attribution took weeks — how confident are we it was actually Russia?"**
> Suggested answer: *"Attribution is now supported by **Polish government**, **NATO** technical analysis, and **multiple independent threat intelligence firms** converging on the same conclusion. The proxy groups involved have documented prior links to Russian military intelligence infrastructure. In cyber terms, this is about as high-confidence as attribution ever gets."*

**Q5: "Is €X (Poland-connected exposure) at risk for us?"** *(the direct exposure question)*
> Suggested answer: *"Our direct exposure to Polish critical infrastructure clients is [TO VALIDATE with internal data]. More material is our **indirect exposure**: Polish corporates in our lending book, cross-border payment volumes, and clients whose supply chains transit Poland. I'll bring a detailed exposure map to the next risk committee."*

**Q6: "You mentioned $30M laundered in minutes. Can we not freeze anything anymore?"**
> Suggested answer: *"Traditional freeze mechanisms depend on identifying the destination account before funds move — and cross-chain crypto bridges collapse that window from hours to seconds. Industry response is moving toward **pre-transaction risk scoring** and **shared threat intel feeds among exchanges** — but the fundamental asymmetry favors the attacker for now."*
