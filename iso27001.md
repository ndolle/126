**Yes—but only in specific, narrowly defined circumstances.** ISO/IEC 27001 certification is **not generally compulsory for all organisations in any European country**, nor is it automatically compulsory for sectors such as banking, healthcare, energy or telecommunications.

ISO itself notes that certification is normally optional, although it can become a **legal or contractual requirement** in particular industries or arrangements. ([ISO][1])

## Position in Europe

| Context                            | Is ISO 27001 certification compulsory? | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **EU organisations generally**     |                                 **No** | There is no EU-wide rule requiring every organisation to hold ISO 27001 certification.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **NIS2 sectors**                   |              **No, not automatically** | NIS2 requires appropriate cybersecurity risk-management measures, incident reporting and management accountability across 18 critical sectors. ISO 27001 can support compliance, but the Directive does not generally require an ISO certificate. ([Digital Strategy EU][2])                                                |
| **EU financial sector under DORA** |                                 **No** | Banks, insurers, investment firms and other regulated financial entities must comply with DORA’s ICT risk-management, testing, incident-management and third-party-risk requirements. ISO 27001 may provide supporting assurance, but it does not replace DORA and is not a general certification requirement. ([EIOPA][3]) |
| **GDPR / UK GDPR**                 |                                 **No** | The obligation is to implement security appropriate to the risk. The UK ICO expressly states that the UK GDPR does not prescribe specific measures and treats ISO 27001 as potential evidence rather than a substitute for legal compliance. ([Information Commissioner's Office][4])                                       |
| **UK NIS-regulated entities**      |                                 **No** | ICO guidance identifies ISO 27001 as an example of an appropriate standard, not as the sole mandatory route. ([Information Commissioner's Office][5])                                                                                                                                                                       |

## European examples where it is, or will be, effectively compulsory

### 1. Spain: private electronic-invoicing platforms

Spain’s Royal Decree 238/2026 states that private electronic-invoicing exchange platforms participating in the Spanish mandatory e-invoicing system must possess:

> ISO/IEC 27001 certification **or an equivalent certification** for their information-security management system.

This is an explicit statutory requirement for that particular category of provider. ([BOE][6])

However, its practical application is deferred until twelve months after the relevant implementing ministerial order enters into force. ([BOE][6])

This is therefore a good example of **sector-specific legal compulsion**, although an equivalent certification may be accepted.

### 2. France: hosting personal health data

Organisations hosting personal health data on behalf of healthcare providers or other third parties generally need **HDS certification**. Healthcare establishments managing their own systems are not necessarily required to be HDS-certified. ([Agence du Numérique en Santé][7])

The compulsory certificate is technically **HDS**, not merely a standalone ISO 27001 certificate. Nevertheless, the HDS framework incorporates the ISO 27001 management-system requirements and applies additional health-data-specific controls. ([Agence du Numérique en Santé][8])

Consequently, ISO 27001 compliance is effectively embedded in the mandatory French certification scheme for covered health-data hosts.

### 3. Italy: Peppol service providers

The Italian Peppol Authority has announced that, from **1 July 2027**, all Peppol service providers must hold a valid and active ISO/IEC 27001 certificate to continue operating on the Peppol network. ([peppol.agid.gov.it][9])

This is better described as a **mandatory network or service-provider qualification requirement**, rather than a general requirement imposed on all Italian businesses.

## When ISO 27001 becomes compulsory without a general law

An organisation may still be obliged to obtain it because:

* a public-sector tender requires ISO 27001 or an equivalent certificate;
* a bank or major customer makes it a supplier condition;
* a licensing, accreditation or market-access scheme incorporates it;
* a group policy requires subsidiaries or critical suppliers to be certified;
* a regulated outsourcing contract expressly requires certification.

In these cases, ISO 27001 is **contractually compulsory**: the organisation may legally exist without it but cannot win or retain the relevant contract, licence, qualification or network membership.

## Important distinction

These statements are not equivalent:

1. **“We follow ISO 27001 principles.”**
   The organisation uses the standard as guidance.

2. **“Our ISMS conforms to ISO 27001.”**
   The organisation claims it has implemented the requirements, potentially without independent certification.

3. **“We are ISO 27001 certified.”**
   An external certification body has audited a defined ISMS scope and issued a certificate.

4. **“ISO 27001 or equivalent is required.”**
   Another recognised certification or assurance mechanism may potentially satisfy the requirement.

Therefore, the practical conclusion is:

> **ISO 27001 is not generally compulsory in Europe, including for banks subject to DORA or organisations subject to NIS2. However, it is compulsory in some narrowly defined national schemes, service-provider roles, licensing arrangements, procurement exercises and commercial contracts.**
