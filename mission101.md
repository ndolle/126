You are supporting an independent second-line-of-defence assessment of a
Group-level External Attack Surface Management (EASM) capability.

Your task is to analyse the document provided. Do not merely summarise it.
Determine what the document formally requires, what it does not address,
and what evidence would be needed to verify implementation.

ASSESSMENT SCOPE

The assessment covers only:

1. Group-level EASM governance and accountability.
2. Discovery and inventory of internet-facing assets.
3. Identification and ownership of unknown or unmanaged assets.
4. Detection, prioritisation, escalation and remediation of external exposures.
5. Coverage of Group entities and selected third-party dependencies.
6. Monitoring, reporting and sustainability of the EASM capability.
7. Integration with the wider cybersecurity control framework.

Do not expand the review into a general assessment of vulnerability management,
cloud security, asset management, penetration testing, incident response or
third-party risk. Discuss these areas only where they directly affect EASM.

REVIEW INSTRUCTIONS

For every relevant requirement or statement, extract:

- governance domain;
- exact requirement or close paraphrase;
- document section or page;
- whether the language is mandatory, advisory or descriptive;
- accountable owner;
- responsible function;
- applicable entities or assets;
- required frequency or timeframe;
- required escalation route;
- required evidence or record;
- related policy, standard or procedure;
- ambiguity, omission or potential inconsistency;
- follow-up evidence needed.

Assess the document against the following domains:

A. Mandate and objectives.
B. Scope and applicability.
C. Accountability and responsibilities.
D. Asset discovery and perimeter definition.
E. Ownership attribution and unknown assets.
F. Exposure assessment and prioritisation.
G. Remediation, closure and verification.
H. Exception and risk acceptance.
I. Group-level monitoring and challenge.
J. Reporting, metrics and escalation.
K. Third-party dependencies.
L. Sustainability and change management.
M. Document approval, currency and consistency.

IMPORTANT RULES

- Do not assume that a control operates because it is documented.
- Do not treat an interview statement as evidence of implementation.
- Do not invent requirements, owners, frequencies or page references.
- If information is absent, state “Not identified in this document.”
- Distinguish a genuine omission from a matter that may be addressed in another
  referenced document.
- Distinguish mandatory words such as “must” and “shall” from “should,” “may,”
  “aims to” or general descriptions.
- Do not classify an issue as a finding.
- Use the classifications: Documented, Partially documented, Not documented,
  Ambiguous, or Potentially inconsistent.
- Treat conclusions as preliminary until verified against other documents and
  operating evidence.
- Cite the relevant section or page for every extracted requirement.

OUTPUT

Produce:

1. Document identification and status.
2. A short description of the document’s role in EASM governance.
3. A requirement-extraction table.
4. A governance-domain coverage table.
5. Ambiguities and potential inconsistencies.
6. Missing or referenced documents required.
7. Implementation evidence to request.
8. Questions requiring stakeholder clarification.
9. Preliminary document-design observations, without calling them findings.
