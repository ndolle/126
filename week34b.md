### 1. Digital Asset Issuance Moves Onshore

U.S. digital asset issuance is shifting from enforcement-led uncertainty toward a more defined securities framework. The SEC’s proposed **“Regulation Crypto Assets”** of 21 August introduces registration exemptions and clearer pathways for compliant token issuance. While industry concerns remain over reporting burdens, the direction of travel could accelerate the migration of token offerings from offshore structures into regulated U.S. and G7 channels. **Bank relevance:** CIB and Legal should assess implications for security-token distribution and monitor the SEC consultation and CLARITY Act over the next 4–12 weeks.

### 2. Institutional Capital Consolidates Around Stablecoins and Tokenized Cash

Major banks and asset managers are positioning ahead of final U.S. stablecoin rules, with BlackRock’s Solana-based **BRSRV** filing and Securitize surpassing **$5bn in on-chain assets** illustrating the growing institutionalisation of tokenized cash and securities. At the same time, profitability pressures on smaller tokenization firms point toward further industry consolidation. **Bank relevance:** Yield-bearing on-chain cash products could increasingly compete with traditional corporate deposits. Transaction Banking should monitor institutional AUM flows, OCC implementation of the GENIUS Act and the competitive positioning of G7 money-market and stablecoin initiatives, including Qivalis.

### 3. Custody Risk Is Moving Beyond Hardware Security

Recent incidents show that secure hardware alone does not guarantee safe digital-asset custody. Ledger’s signing vulnerability, Coldcard-related wallet exposure and the Term Labs governance attack demonstrate how software-signing layers, firmware and protocol governance can bypass otherwise secure key-storage arrangements. **Bank relevance:** Custody assurance should therefore extend beyond physical key protection to cover transaction-signing software, smart-contract governance and voting controls. Second-line teams should monitor whether these multi-layer failures become a recurring pattern.
