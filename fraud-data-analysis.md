Yes, and I think this is actually an important point because the three infographics collectively tell a story that is not immediately obvious when looking at the raw data.

I would describe them as follows:

---

## 1. Wholesale, Corporate and Institutional Fraud

### "The Control Failure Layer"

This infographic primarily captures what happens when institutions themselves fail.

The dominant themes are:

* AML failures
* Transaction monitoring deficiencies
* Suspicious activity reporting failures
* Governance failures
* Regulatory enforcement

The focus is not on customers losing money directly, but on regulators identifying weaknesses that allowed criminal activity to occur or go undetected.

### Key Message

> The largest institutional risks are increasingly arising from failures in governance, reporting, monitoring, and financial crime controls rather than from individual fraud events themselves.

### Audience

* Board Risk Committees
* CRO
* Head of Financial Crime
* Internal Audit
* Regulators

### What Makes It Valuable

It provides insight into:

* How peer institutions are failing
* What regulators are prioritising
* Which control weaknesses are attracting major penalties

In many ways, this infographic is a forward-looking indicator of regulatory expectations.

---

## 2. Commercial, Retail and Digital Payments Fraud

### "The Customer Impact Layer"

This infographic focuses on where money is actually being lost.

The dominant themes are:

* APP fraud
* Card fraud
* Payment fraud
* Identity theft
* Deepfake scams
* Impersonation scams

This is where the largest aggregate losses appear.

Examples:

* £576m APP fraud (UK)
* €4.2bn EEA payment fraud
* $15.9bn FTC consumer fraud
* $20.9bn FBI internet crime losses

### Key Message

> Fraud is increasingly becoming a psychological attack problem rather than a technical attack problem.

The data repeatedly shows that criminals are persuading customers to authenticate fraudulent transactions themselves.

### Audience

* Retail Banking
* Fraud Operations
* Customer Protection Teams
* Digital Banking
* Payments Functions

### What Makes It Valuable

It shows:

* Where customers are losing money
* Which payment rails are being exploited
* How criminals are adapting to strong authentication controls

This infographic is arguably the best measure of current fraud pressure.

---

## 3. Wealth Management, Insurance and Investment Fraud

### "The Manipulation Layer"

This infographic focuses on deception.

The dominant themes are:

* Investment scams
* Crypto fraud
* AI-washing
* Ponzi schemes
* Fiduciary breaches
* Misappropriation of investor funds

What stands out is that the fraud often begins long before money moves.

The attacker first creates:

* Trust
* Credibility
* Artificial legitimacy

Only then does the financial loss occur.

### Key Message

> The modern investment fraud landscape is increasingly driven by the industrialisation of trust manipulation.

The emergence of AI-washing is particularly notable because it represents a completely new fraud category that barely existed a few years ago.

### Audience

* Wealth Management
* Private Banking
* Asset Management
* Investor Protection Teams
* Conduct Risk

### What Makes It Valuable

It highlights:

* Emerging fraud narratives
* Investor manipulation techniques
* New forms of securities and crypto fraud

This infographic provides a view of where future fraud trends may emerge before they appear in traditional banking fraud statistics.

# The Most Interesting Observation

When viewed together, the three infographics form a natural hierarchy:

### Layer 1 — Institutional Failure

(Wholesale & Corporate)

**Question:**
"How are institutions failing?"

Examples:

* AML failures
* Monitoring failures
* Reporting failures

---

### Layer 2 — Customer Harm

(Retail & Payments)

**Question:**
"Where is money being stolen today?"

Examples:

* APP fraud
* Identity theft
* Deepfake scams

---

### Layer 3 — Fraud Evolution

(Wealth & Investment)

**Question:**
"What are criminals likely to do next?"

Examples:

* AI-washing
* Crypto scams
* Synthetic investment opportunities

---

# Strategic Takeaway

If presented together, the three infographics create something much more powerful than a fraud dashboard.

They effectively provide three complementary perspectives:

| Infographic                          | Answers                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Wholesale, Corporate & Institutional | How institutions fail    |
| Commercial, Retail & Payments        | How customers lose money |
| Wealth, Insurance & Investment       | How criminals evolve     |

That is why I think the three-part structure works so well: it mirrors the way a second-line risk function naturally thinks about financial crime—**control failures, realised losses, and emerging threats**.
